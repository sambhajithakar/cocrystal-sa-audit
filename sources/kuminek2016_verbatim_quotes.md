## Definitions and relations (quoted)

> "The cocrystal solubility advantage, SA, is introduced as a dimensionless solubility number to
> characterize the ability of a given cocrystal to alter the solubility of a drug at a given pH,
> solubilizing agent concentration, temperature, etc"

> "The solubility of a cocrystal is not a unique value determined by its solid-state chemisty, but
> a series of values determined by what happens to cocrystal molecules when they dissolve."

> "Plots of log (SR) vs log (SR) produce straight lines with slopes of 1/2 for 1:1 cocrystals and
> 2/3 for 2:1 cocrystals"

i.e. **SR_cocrystal = SR_drug^(1/2)** (1:1) and **SR_cocrystal = SR_drug^(2/3)** (2:1, printed as
Eq. 8).

> "The plot of log (SA) vs log (SR) is characterized by: (1) lines with slope of -1/2 where the
> position of each line is determined by the cocrystal SA value"

i.e. for 1:1, **SA_observed = SA_aqueous x SR_drug^(-1/2)**, so
**SA_aqueous = SA_observed x SR_drug^(1/2)**. This is the harmonization relation.

> "A central assumption of these simple relationships is that coformer is not solubilized by
> additives. ... Positive deviations observed for several 1:1 cocrystals at high values of SR are a
> result of coformer solubilization and can be quantified by a factor epsilon"

> "where SA is the total cocrystal solubility at drug solubilization SR, and SA is the aqueous
> cocrystal solubility advantage in the absence of drug solubilization. When SR=1, SA=SA in the
> absence of drug solubilization."

Cocrystal solubility for a 1:1 cocrystal of a non-ionizable drug with an ionizable coformer
(printed as Eq. 5): **S_cocrystal = sqrt( Ksp (1 + 10^(pH - pKa,coformer)) )**

## Statements that frame the problem

> "Even the cocrystal solubility advantage over drug (S/S) can change and be reversed as cocrystal
> components ionize and interact with endogenous or formulation additives to different extents."

> "cocrystals exhibit transition points at which the cocrystal and drug solubilities are equal, and
> above or below which the cocrystal solubility advantage over drug is eliminated (SA<=1)"

> "Cocrystal aqueous solubility has been reported to be as high as 1,000 times the drug solubility"

> "Unlike the commonly used descriptor of cocrystal kinetic solubility, cocrystal eutectic constants
> provide a value whereby cocrystals can be ranked in terms of their solubility advantage over drug.
> A strong case is made for eutectic constants to be measured as the most important cocrystal
> property"

> "Thus there is generally an empirical search for cocrystal formulation excipients, dissolution
> additives, and even crystallization inhibitors that require a large number of experiments with,
> little if any, translation to new situations."

BCS, quoted in full:

> "Cocrystals can generate supersaturation with respect to the less soluble parent drug, and solve
> bioavailability problems in particular for Biopharmaceutical Classification System (BCS) class II
> drugs (low aqueous solubility, and high permeability). Pharmacokinetic studies of about 60
> cocrystals have been reported and while there is clear improvement on bioavailability for many
> cocrystals, our limited understanding of their solution and permeability behavior prevents more
> pharmaceutical cocrystals from being used in drug products"
